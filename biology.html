Observing an Onion Peel Cell

Have you ever wondered what a living thing looks like under a microscope?
Although we cannot see cells with our naked eyes, they are the basic structural and functional units of life. In this experiment, we use a microscope to observe the cells present in a thin layer of an onion peel.
🔬 The Onion Peel Experiment
An onion is made up of many tiny cells arranged closely together. By carefully removing a thin layer from the inner surface of an onion and placing it on a glass slide, we can observe
these cells under a microscope.
When viewed under the microscope, the cells appear brick-like and closely packed, giving us a clear view of their basic structure.

🎯 Aim of the Experiment

To prepare a temporary slide of an onion peel and observe its cells under a microscope.

🧪 Materials Required

Onion • Microscope • Glass slide • Coverslip • Dropper • Water • Stain • Needle/forceps
🔍 Procedure
1. Take a small piece of onion and carefully remove a thin inner peel.
2. Place the peel on a clean glass slide.
3. Add a drop of water and a suitable stain.
4. Gently place a coverslip over the peel.
5.Place the slide under the microscope.
6.Observe the cells under low and then high magnification.
👀 What Do We Observe?

Under the microscope, we can observe:

Cell wall — gives the cell its definite shape.
Cell membrane — lies just inside the cell wall.
Cytoplasm — jelly-like material inside the cell.
Nucleus — controls the activities of the cell.
Large vacuole — occupies much of the cell's interior.

The onion peel cells appear rectangular or brick-shaped and are arranged closely together.

💡 What Does This Experiment Teach Us?

This simple observation gives us a glimpse into the microscopic world of living organisms and helps us understand the cell as the basic unit of life.
