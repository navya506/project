📐 Sum of Exterior Angles of a Regular Polygon
🎯 Aim
To show that the sum of the exterior angles of any regular polygon is always 360°, no matter how many sides it has.

🧰 Materials Required

Ruler
Protractor
Pencil and coloured pens
Plain sheets of paper
Compass (for drawing regular polygons)

❓ What is an Exterior Angle?
When one side of a polygon is extended, the angle formed between the extended side and the next side is called an exterior angle. At every corner (vertex) of a polygon, there is one exterior angle. 📏

🔍 Procedure

Draw a regular polygon (like an equilateral triangle, square, pentagon, and hexagon) on separate sheets. ✏️
At each vertex, extend one side outward to form the exterior angle.
Measure each exterior angle using a protractor. 📐
Add up all the exterior angles of the same polygon.
Repeat for polygons with different numbers of sides and record your readings.

💡 Why This Happens
Imagine walking all the way around the boundary of a polygon and coming back to your starting point, facing the same direction you began with. 🚶 At every corner, you turn through the exterior angle. Since you make one complete turn by the time you return to the start, all the turns (exterior angles) must add up to one full turn — that's 360°! 🔄
This is true for any polygon — regular or irregular — but in a regular polygon, all exterior angles are equal, so each one measures exactly 360° ÷ n, where n is the number of sides.
